## Proposed portfolio architecture

Homepage: WizPay as the focused case study; Building WizCommerce as the collection. Preserve Superr's shared reading style. The collection starts with short company/contribution context and a visible linked index. Each chapter has a stable anchor. A reader can browse continuously or jump to one topic.

### Featured: WizPay

Use a short collection preview leading to the dedicated study. Combine the backstory with the old portfolio's modularity explanation and carefully selected visual evidence. Avoid repeating the entire study inside the collection. Keep historical metrics out until their date, denominator and source are confirmed.

### 1. Helping reps find and choose products

Combine smart search, smart collections and product cards with the old sales-data story. Order: find a product → consider relevant alternatives → inspect information needed to choose it.

The old page supplies concrete decisions for product cards: inventory/ETA placement, variant-related CTA behavior, and optional promotional information. Select one before/after and one supporting detail rather than repeat every device. Purchase-history, cart and variant-sheet designs are supporting examples of the same information moving through the journey.

Smart collections appears related to the older recommendation-grouping work, but the old page labels that work WIP. Confirm the relationship and chronology before presenting a before/after or claiming it shipped. Search remains its own short subsection; the current visual alone does not establish its design rationale.

### 2. Keeping work moving through interruptions

Combine offline mode and notification cards. Give each a distinct subsection.

Offline: old story explains blocking initial sync, uncertainty about readiness, and staged sync. Separate the engineering change from the interface work. Pair a short flow illustration with a screen showing readiness/progress.

Notifications: combine the Figma download states with the old category/state/action breakdown and device behavior. Show how one card changes across in-progress, failed and ready states without repeating the whole notification panel.

Old time-saving and adoption numbers require confirmation before reuse.

### 3. Making communication part of the order

Add the email work as the ninth piece; it is not present in the eight-piece board. This deserves a more substantial compact story because it contains alternatives and reasons for rejecting them.

Suggested sequence: input looked complete when it was not → recipient selection was easy to miss → hierarchy-only and interruptive alternatives → an integrated checkpoint → a brief look at organization-level controls.

Use the input comparison, a rejected/final flow comparison and one settings image. Treat the old page's research/behavior claims as the user's published account; do not invent testing details. Describe historical trigger counts only with their original scope.

### 4. Configuring the everyday work

Custom discounts and label management remain two distinct short stories in one chapter. The Figma visuals establish the surfaces, but the reason for the design decisions is not yet supplied.

Candidate questions: how did discount eligibility affect the setup flow? How did label formats, attributes and defaults affect management? Use answers to select one decision and one useful visual per story. Do not infer these requirements solely from field labels.

## Editorial depth

- Main case study: WizPay, built around three major decisions.
- More substantial collection sections: product decisions, offline mode and email workflows.
- Shorter sections: search, smart collections, notifications, discounts and labels. Expand only when a distinct decision warrants it.
- Each item: situation, one decision with its reason, selected visual evidence, and an outcome or honest limitation when available.

Retain useful original artifacts and concrete decisions. Rewrite repeated company introductions, broad industry claims, feature inventories and generic reflections. Do not force all pieces into equal-length case studies.
