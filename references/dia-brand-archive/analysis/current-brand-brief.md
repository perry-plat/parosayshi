# Dia now — current brand brief

**Observation date:** 2026-08-03
**Weighting:** current system first; history as lineage.

## The brand in one sentence

Dia currently sells relief from the fragmented workday: the browser quietly carries context across tabs, turns it into useful synthesis, and gives the user a calmer sense of readiness.

## What is most distinctive now

1. **Emotion before mechanism.** The homepage opens on dread and a human scream, not an AI feature diagram.
2. **Context continuity as the product story.** Tabs, meetings, groups, reports, memory, and work recur more than model vocabulary.
3. **Authored outputs over feature tiles.** Reports, briefs, notes, and editorial objects make the intelligence feel tangible.
4. **A loud opening, then calm proof.** One conspicuous gesture gives way to pale backgrounds, disciplined typography, and browser-frame evidence.
5. **Editorial display plus utility systems.** Exposure Variable carries expression; ABC Oracle carries product copy; ABC Favorit Mono and Quadrant Text Mono carry metadata.
6. **Color lives in content.** The foundation is near-black, white, and `#F8F8F8`; chroma appears inside reports, skills, illustrations, and campaign worlds.
7. **Shipping is culture.** Dia Weekly turns release notes into a recurring publication with issue numbers, dates, locations, app versions, builder credits, art, and personal recommendations.
8. **Second person dominates.** Across 26,412 words of filtered public brand and release copy, second-person language appears 1,271 times versus 294 first-person-plural uses; contractions appear 656 times.

## Current visual grammar

- Floating pill navigation that behaves like browser chrome.
- Large editorial serif headlines beside quiet sans-serif product explanation.
- Mono labels for dates, issues, versions, navigation, and provenance.
- Very pale page grounds, soft glow fields, round browser surfaces, and restrained shadows.
- Human documentary scenes, authored report surfaces, product screenshots, paper/postcard motifs, and credited art.
- Pinned or staged scroll sequences with one dominant visual transformation per viewport.

## Current voice formula

1. Name a familiar emotional or practical friction.
2. Frame Dia as the continuity layer already present in the browser.
3. Show a useful output rather than describing intelligence abstractly.
4. Resolve in an ordinary human benefit: readiness, less hunting, a better day.
5. Add privacy, control, or security proof when the claim becomes consequential.
6. End with a low-friction invitation.

## Borrow the grammar, not the skin

For Parosayshi, the transferable ideas are the contrast between expressive opening and calm evidence, mono provenance on tactile case-study objects, authored output cards, and editorialized release history. Avoid copying Dia’s licensed typefaces, exact navigation pill, scream asset, product screenshots, or sentence-level voice.
